# Phase 5

## Q-only predictive analysis
### Figure
The key observations are:
- The meta-only model already explains about $R^2$ ≈0.094 on held-out data.
- Adding Q-norm raises this substantially to roughly 0.187.
- Adding Q-direction information gives about 0.164.
- A Q-only predictive setup achieves AUROC ≈0.67.

### Observation
Query-side quantities contain substantial predictive information about the frozen sink score, and query norm provides a particularly large incremental contribution over the baseline features.

### Interpretation
Sink specialization is associated with the state of the query pathway, not solely with the identity or position of the sink key.

### Conclusion
The query pathway contains predictive information about sink specialization, motivating a direct mechanistic test of the Q pathway.

It does not establish "Q causes the sink nor Q alone determines the sink". It's predictive evidence, not causal evidence.

## K structure by position
### Figure
The earliest positions have cross-prompt K cosine≈1 and very large K norms, while the values become less stable and smaller as position increases

### Observation
Key representations have strong positional structure: the earliest key positions are unusually stable across prompts and have large K norms.

Especially position 0 and 1 are extremely stable, K norm drops sharply after the first few positions.

### Interpretation
The key pathway also contains strong positional structure around the earliest positions, so a query-only account would be incomplete without considering the highly structured key environment in which the query operates.

### Conclusion
The sink arises in an asymmetric Q/K setting: the query pathway varies in a head-dependent way, while the key pathway has unusually strong positional structure at early positions.

## QK factorial intervention
### Figure

The 4 configurations differ QK independently:(Qs​,Ks​),(Qs​,Kn​),(Qn​,Ks​),(Qn​,Kn​).

The cells differ strongly, and the interaction estimate is: $I_{qk}$ ≈ 1.67

with bootstrap CI around: [1.557,1.790].
### Observation
Changing the query and key components independently changes the sink margin, and the factorial contrast yields a robust nonzero Q×K interaction.

### Interpretation
Sink preference is generated by an interaction between query-side and key-side representations rather than being attributable to either component in isolation.

### Conclusion
The Q–K interaction is mechanistically relevant to sink-margin formation.

## QK interaction vs position
### Figure
The mean $I_{qk}$ varies somewhat across query-position bins, but the uncertainty is very large.

And the natural vs position-matched comparison is essentially identical
### Observation
The measured Q×K interaction does not show a clear systematic dependence on the sampled query-position bins, and position matching produces nearly the same aggregate interaction estimate.

### Interpretation
The QK interaction is not simply an artifact of the natural distribution of query positions in the sampled data.

### Conclusion
The observed Q×K interaction survives position matching, supporting it as a genuine mechanistic quantity rather than a trivial positional sampling artifact.

## Softmax/statistical null
###Figure
The null model already reaches approximately $R^2$≈0.41.

The mechanistic model gives only: $R^2$ ≈0.43,

so: $ΔR^2$ ≈0.017.

### Observation
Generic/statistical logit and competition features already explain a large fraction of sink-margin variation, while the explicitly mechanistic feature set provides only a small incremental improvement.

### Interpretation
Finding a mechanistically meaningful variable is not equivalent to finding a sufficient explanation of sink behaviour.

The Q×K interaction is real.

But once generic quantities such as sink logit, competitor statistics, etc. are known, the additional explanatory value of the mechanistic feature set is modest.

### Conclusion
The sink margin is substantially predictable from generic logit/competition structure, and the tested mechanistic variables do not provide a complete or dramatically superior static explanation.

## QK interaction across depth
### Figure
The QK interaction varies substantially with depth, including large spikes around some layers.

So $I_{qk}$ is emphatically not static.

### Observation
The magnitude of QK interaction changes strongly across transformer depth, with pronounced layer-specific peaks.

### Interpretation
The mechanism generating the attention logits itself evolves across the network.

But note: mechanism evolving does not yet mean sink evolving according to that mechanism.

### Conclusion
QK interaction is depth-dependent, indicating that the underlying logit-generation geometry changes substantially across layers.

## QK interaction vs sink strength
### Observation
QK interaction varies across depth, but its magnitude does not track sink strength across the tested layers/conditions.

### Interpretation
The mechanism varies but sink trajectory does not simply follow the mechanism’s magnitude. A scalar description is inadequate in this case

### Conclusion
Although QK interaction is mechanistically relevant, its magnitude does not provide a simple static account of the layer-wise sink-strength trajectory.

## Normalised trajectory comparison
### Observation
The normalised $I_{qk}$ vs normalised sink strength trajectories are clearly not locked together.

The sink-strength curve has a fairly coherent upward/plateauing trend toward later layers while the QK interaction curve oscillates dramatically.

### Interpretation
The evolving logit interaction geometry does not map onto the sink-strength trajectory through a simple one-dimensional relationship.

### Conclusion
A static or single-feature mechanistic description of the sink is insufficient to explain how sink strength evolves across depth.

## Working conclusion
The sink has a genuine QK interaction mechanism, but the magnitude of that interaction does not provide a simple explanation of how sink strength evolves across depth. Generic logit/competition structure explains much of the observed sink-margin variation, while explicitly mechanistic features add only limited predictive power. 
Thus, although the ingredients of attention logit formation are mechanistically relevant, a static description of those ingredients does not fully account for the observed sink trajectory.
